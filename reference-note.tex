\keyboxtitle{A note on the open specification}
Because HISA is a \emph{specification}, a description of operations and how they compose,
it can be written down, versioned, and refined the way engineers maintain a real
instruction set. An evolving draft is published openly as a companion to this book,
modelled on the manuals of real architectures such as the DEC PDP-11 and RISC-V. It lists
the base living-organism instruction set (LISA), the human standard extensions (language,
reasoning, self-reference, volition, time-binding, affect, and the proposed spiritual
channel), a vector extension describing the human as a massively parallel machine beyond
any silicon design, and the microarchitecture, compiler, and operating-system layers.
Readers who wish to analyse, critique, or extend the model, or simply to see the argument
of this chapter in full engineering detail, are invited to read and contribute.

\medskip
\noindent The specification is deliberately unfinished, and marks throughout what is
established \textbf{[A]}, what is a defensible model \textbf{[B]}, and what is speculative
\textbf{[C]}, the same discipline this book keeps.
